## 1 · Opening (0–7 s): the macrae logo comes alive, orbits warp, camera dives into the sun

> What we built is a research assistant, for Pavel Jungwirth's group at the Institute of Organic Chemistry and
> Biochemistry in Prague.

## 2 · The problem (7–25 s): the lab, re-typing context, RAG

> Our colleagues are quantum chemists, biologists, statisticians. They are not software engineers. They lose hours
> teaching models like Claude their own context.
>
> So we taught our research assistant what our lab has worked on. We sourced a hundred papers.
>
> And since models get weaker as you fill the context, it retrieves only what's relevant.

## 3 · Why it matters (25–31 s)

> That saves them tens of hours. It saves your money too, because they're paid from public taxes.

## 4 · Pavel Jungwirth (31–45 s)

> We also did this for Pavel, the leader of the group. We were saddened by his passing, and we want to celebrate his
> life: by expanding on his research, and helping his students build more, faster.

## 5 · What it does (45–60 s): a real run, fast-forwarded, the paper written in parallel

> We give his research questions. It produces what scientists do: code, results, and papers.
>
> It knows the literature, decides what to run and where, and runs real jobs. Today it lives on Cloudflare. Soon, on
> IOCB's cluster, for the whole lab.

_(on screen: the plan deciding what and where, the trace filling in, costs in $ ticking, the manuscript typed with
deletions, figures, formulas, citations)_

## 6 · Frankenstein: it learns (60–73 s)

> tests it, installs it, and uses it again. Its skills evolve.

_(on screen: notes-to-self; the real example: early runs reinstalled the same packages and hit a broken Python
environment, so it built a ready-made, version-pinned environment, tested it, installed it, used it next run)_

_(on screen: the galaxy turns from dusk to dawn, the sun brightens with each capability, then the measured
